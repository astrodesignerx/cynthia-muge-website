For the translator

These are public comments about a Nandi County politician, collected for research. We need to understand what was actually said — not a polished version.

  • Literal first, then meaning. Give the word-for-word sense, then what it actually conveys to a Nandi listener.
  • Tell us the register. Is it affectionate, neutral, teasing, dismissive, insulting, or seriously offensive? That distinction is the whole point of the exercise.
  • Don't soften anything. If a line is crude or demeaning, say so plainly. A cleaned-up translation is worse than useless here.
  • Flag ambiguity. If a line could be read two ways, or if spelling is non-standard and you are guessing, say which reading you chose and why.
  • Note if it is not Nandi. Some lines mix Swahili, Sheng or English. Say which language each part is.
  • Gendered usage matters most. Where a term refers to a woman, tell us whether it is ordinary, patronising, or an insult — and whether it would be used about a man.

Spelling is as posted, including errors. Please do not correct it — the misspellings may themselves be informative.

One non-language question, for the same person

Two councils of elders appear in the research. The Talai Council of Elders has publicly blessed Allan Kosgey's candidacy. Cynthia Muge attended the installation of the National Chairman of the Nandi Kaburwo Council of Elders.

Which council carries more weight in Nandi, and on what? Are they rivals, complementary, or unrelated? An endorsement from one may mean far more than the other, and we currently have no way to judge.

Also worth asking: is there a name behind "Mobutu Sese Seko", used as a derogatory label for a candidate in two posts?
