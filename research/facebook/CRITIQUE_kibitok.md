# THE SHARPEST CRITIQUE IN THE ENTIRE HARVEST
Robert Kibitok (shared by Rotich Ben) · NEW NANDIHILLS CONSTITUENCY PROGRESS · Aug 2026
FULL TEXT — this is the attack the documentary exists to answer.

"Cynthia Muge: BEFORE WE TALK ABOUT THE GOVERNOR'S SEAT, CAN WE AUDIT THE WOMAN REP'S SEAT?

I have been watching the conversation around your gubernatorial ambitions. Personally,
what I have seen from the Woman Representative's office has not convinced me that you are
ready for a bigger responsibility. But let me leave politics aside for a moment.

Let's talk about two programmes that have received a lot of attention: Mama na Kahawa and
sports.

MAMA NA KAHAWA
We hear big numbers of seedlings distributed. But here is what I want to know:
  How many seedlings came from NGAAF? How many came from the National Government?
  Who supplied them?
  At what price?
  Which women's groups received them?
  Which wards benefited?

And the biggest question:
  IF YOU GIVE A WOMEN'S GROUP COFFEE SEEDLINGS BUT THERE IS NO FACTORY OR CLEAR MARKET
  AROUND THEM, WHAT HAPPENS WHEN THOSE TREES MATURE?
  Do we just celebrate the planting ceremony and leave the farmer to figure out the rest?

And what about the Nandi woman who doesn't need coffee?
  The mama mboga? The boda boda rider? The small trader? The unemployed graduate?
  The young person without a degree? The PWD?
  Where is their empowerment programme?

THEN SPORTS.
Football and volleyball are good. But Nandi has a serious unemployment and economic
problem. So I ask:
  How much are we spending on tournaments?
  How many young people get a sustainable livelihood after the final whistle?
  What happens to the thousands of young people who aren't sportsmen and women?

This is where I think we need to wake up.
  Empowerment is not the number of seedlings planted.
  Empowerment is not the number of tournaments held.
  Empowerment is when a mother has a business. A young person has an income. A graduate
  gets an opportunity. A PWD is included. A farmer has a market. And a public programme
  can show us exactly where the money went and exactly who benefited.

I am Not convinced by your work when you were an MCA and so when you are the Woman
representative.

BRING THE NUMBERS. BRING THE BENEFICIARY LISTS. BRING THE PROCUREMENT RECORDS. BRING THE
IMPACT.

If the evidence proves me wrong, I will say support your gubernatorial ambitions.
Until then, LET'S STOP CONFUSING VISIBILITY WITH IMPACT.
Nandi deserves to ask. And leaders deserve to answer."

## WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN ANYTHING ELSE FOUND
1. It is SOBER. No insults, no gender, no culture. Impossible to dismiss as propaganda.
2. It is SPECIFIC. Procurement, suppliers, prices, beneficiary lists, ward breakdowns.
3. It pre-empts the campaign's two flagship programmes simultaneously.
4. It names the ONE HOLE THE RESEARCH HAD NOT IDENTIFIED:
   >>> THE MARKET QUESTION. Seedlings without a factory or buyer produce worthless trees.
   >>> The documentary covers a cooperative weighing/paying ONE farmer. It does NOT
       establish whether Nandi has processing and market capacity for 1.5M trees.
       THIS IS NOW THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION IN THE WHOLE PROJECT.
5. It names the BREADTH PROBLEM: her signature programme is narrow by design. Coffee
   helps coffee farmers. Mama mboga, boda riders, traders, graduates, PWDs are not served
   by it. "Where is their empowerment programme?"

## WHAT IT VALIDATES
- The film's evidence-first thesis, the sourced end cards, the honest column (Scene 14 /
  NW12), and the data annex (D6) are ALL now directly responsive to a real, named,
  circulating critique rather than a hypothetical one.
- "Bring the beneficiary lists. Bring the procurement records." is almost word-for-word
  the film's evidence protocol.

## WHAT MUST CHANGE
- The documentary needs a MARKET scene: is there a factory? a buyer? at what price? for
  how much volume? Interview a cooperative manager and AFA on absorptive capacity.
- The shorts series needs at least one short answering the breadth question, or the
  campaign concedes every non-coffee voter to whoever answers it first.
