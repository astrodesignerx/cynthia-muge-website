# Copy Voice Guide

**Purpose:** Keep the website warm, confident, specific, and credible.
**Audience:** Residents of Nandi, families, young people, programme participants, supporters, journalists, and people meeting Cynthia Muge for the first time.
**Working principle:** Let the work speak.

## The Reading Order

Every page should move through this order:

1. Welcome the reader into Nandi and the subject.
2. Name the person, place, or community.
3. Describe the work with a direct verb.
4. Explain why the work matters in ordinary language.
5. Place the source and date quietly beneath the claim.
6. Describe the next update as something the office is following.

The site should feel like an open door into the work, not a response to an argument.

## Voice

### Be warm

Use the language of home, place, participation, care, and shared responsibility.

Good:

> At Chepkemel, two local groups received support to put their plans into action.

Avoid:

> The public record names the groups and the amount.

### Be confident

State what happened without explaining why the reader should believe it.

Good:

> The office secured a county allocation for ten bulk milk coolers.

Avoid:

> This one is national money, not hers.

### Be specific

Use names, wards, dates, institutions, and amounts when the source supports them.

Good:

> At Lelmokwo Centre, a completed boda boda shade gives riders a place to wait and work.

Avoid:

> Infrastructure is being transformed across the county.

### Be honest without sounding apologetic

Use a forward-looking update rather than a prosecution-style gap.

Good:

> The next update will follow how the groups use the grants.

Avoid:

> It is not evidence of what the money produced.

## Page Copy Structure

### Welcome

Introduce the person and the place. This is where warmth belongs.

### Work

Describe the action and the people involved. Use active verbs such as delivered, supported, registered, trained, secured, hosted, and planted.

### Meaning

Explain the practical reason for the work. Keep it close to everyday life.

### Source

Show the publisher and date beneath the number or statement. The source line is part of the design, not a paragraph in the argument.

### Next update

Use calm labels such as:

- What we are following
- The next update
- Information being gathered
- Awaiting confirmation
- The next responsibility

## Words And Phrases

Prefer:

- work
- service
- support
- people
- families
- groups
- neighbours
- residents
- underway
- delivered
- growing
- taking shape
- being gathered
- next update
- documented

Avoid in visible narrative copy:

- rebuttal
- charge
- vulnerability
- proof, when used as a challenge to a critic
- not hers
- not just
- against her graphic
- the question is
- the public record names
- it is not evidence
- what nobody has published
- doing something harder than
- without having to argue
- what is still being counted, as a headline

These terms may remain in internal research, source notes, or technical status labels when they are needed for accuracy.

## Evidence States

The data model keeps its strict states:

- `verified`
- `unconfirmed`
- `unmeasured`

The public presentation should be gentler:

- `verified` displays as `Documented`
- `unconfirmed` displays as `Awaiting confirmation`
- `unmeasured` displays as `Being gathered`

The meaning must not change. A softer label is not permission to publish an unsupported number.

## Headline Rules

- Headlines should describe the work, not the argument around it.
- Use a person, place, or concrete object where possible.
- Use no more than one big idea in a headline.
- Keep political ambition in the vision and biography sections, not in every programme description.
- Let a source line carry the technical detail.

## Before And After

Current:

> The handover is counted. The life of each tree is the question that follows.

Preferred:

> At Koilot, 22,000 coffee seedlings began their journey into the hands of farmers. Future updates will follow the trees into their first harvest.

Current:

> A team was disqualified, and it was announced.

Preferred:

> At Chepkunyuk, the tournament rules were applied and the result was published with the points adjusted.

Current:

> What makes it different

Preferred:

> How the work takes shape

Current:

> Still to be counted

Preferred:

> What we are following next

## Final Read-Aloud Test

Before publishing a page, ask:

- Does the opening make a resident feel welcomed into a real place?
- Does the copy show Cynthia's strengths through actions rather than praise?
- Would the page still sound natural if the source line were removed?
- Does every number have a source or a clear status?
- Does any sentence sound like it is answering an opponent?
- Does the next update feel like continued service rather than an admission of failure?
- Would a resident recognise the language as human and local rather than campaign jargon?
