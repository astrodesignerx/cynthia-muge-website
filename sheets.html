Sheet 01  ·  Film and photography

The Film

A documentary record of Kahawa na Mama, built around the moment a woman is paid in her own name.

Starts Sept
Delivers January

Why it matters

Every figure attached to the programme counts what was given out. None counts what came back. That is the difference between a giveaway and a record, and it is the gap opponents are already pointing at in public.

Trees planted in 2023 are bearing now. The three-year before-and-after can be filmed this season and never again.

What gets built

  • A 24-minute documentary following four farmers across the programme, from nursery to payment slip
  • A shorter hall cut for rallies, churches and community screenings where 24 minutes will not hold
  • A testimony library of 30+ full interviews, archived and indexed by name, ward and phase
  • A stills archive for print, press and the website
  • An honest section on what did not work — the trees that died and why
Timeline
Sept cast and verify · Oct–Dec filming, tied to the harvest · Jan release and screening tour
What I need
Introduction to four or five farmers and to the cooperative. Any 2023 photographs of planted plots. Permission to film freely.
Executed by
Me, personally — camera, sound, interviews, edit, subtitles, grade.
Done looks like

A film screened in six sub-counties before it goes online, and a permanent archive of farmers on the record in their own words.

Sheet 02  ·  Capture and publishing

Owning the Footage

Filming every public appearance in full, and publishing the same day, so the clip that travels is yours.

Starts Sept
Runs continuously

Why it matters

Four of the five most-watched clips of you speaking were uploaded by other accounts, reaching 216,300, 215,200, 186,300 and 149,800 views. Your own best post reached 80,500.

Those edits carry no context and were not cleared with anyone. Every controversy on record spread through a clip someone else cut. Owning the footage is the structural fix, not a statement after the fact.

What gets built

  • Full-length capture at every public appearance, with proper sound
  • A four-hour turnaround from event to published clip, with context intact
  • A YouTube archive of complete speeches, so journalists quote the whole thing
  • A rapid-response capability for the moment something is taken out of context
Timeline
Can begin immediately. Needs no film crew and no budget approval to start.
What I need
Access to the diary far enough ahead to be there. Permission to film events that go badly as well as well.
Executed by
Me on the ground, with a field edit setup so publishing does not wait for an office.
Done looks like

The next time a phrase of yours is clipped and circulated, the full version is already online with the date on it.

Sheet 03  ·  Content and digital

The Weekly Rhythm

One short video a week until nominations, each ending on a single fact with its source on screen.

Oct – Jan
16 weeks

Why it matters

Posts published with a caption reach 80,500. The same account without one reaches 802. That is not a strategy problem, it is a workflow problem, and it is the cheapest fix available.

A weekly rhythm also builds an audience before the film lands, so that in January these are not strangers.

What gets built

  • Sixteen shorts, one a week, in four movements: establish, evidence, argument, case
  • A format that repeats — a fixed opening mark, burned-in subtitles, a sourced fact card to close
  • A caption system written differently for Facebook and TikTok, because the audiences differ
  • A distribution list of ward coordinators, because a page post does not reach the groups on its own
  • Weekly measurement on share rate and completion, not vanity view counts
Timeline
First short out in week one of October. Weekly through to nominations.
What I need
One person in the office who owns publishing with me. Access to the account, not a hand-off each time.
Executed by
Me — shoot, edit, caption, schedule, publish, measure.
Done looks like

Sixteen weeks of material that can each be checked, and an audience that recognises the format before it sees the name.

Sheet 04  ·  Graphic design and brand

One Visual Identity

A single look, applied everywhere, so anything published is recognisable as yours before the name is read.

Sept
Then applied throughout

Why it matters

Across four Facebook presences, TikTok, X and the website there is no shared visual language. Nothing you post can be identified by sight. Meanwhile supporter pages produce material in a dozen different styles, all attributed to you.

An identity is also protection: material that looks official is harder to counterfeit and easier to trust.

What gets built

  • A core system — one mark, one type pairing, one colour set, defined properly
  • Templates for the fact cards, quote cards, event graphics and lower-thirds
  • An audio mark for video, so a clip is recognisable with the sound on and the screen off
  • A short guide supporter pages can follow, turning scattered goodwill into consistent output
  • Print assets for banners, tents and programme materials
Timeline
Two to three weeks in September, then it costs nothing to keep using.
What I need
A decision on which Facebook presences consolidate, and sign-off on one direction rather than a committee.
Note
Any branding placed on cooperative property needs legal clearance first. Programme names are safer than personal ones.
Done looks like

Someone scrolling past recognises the post as yours before reading a word of it.

Sheet 06  ·  Data visualisation

Numbers People Understand

Turning official records into charts that make an argument in three seconds.

Rolling
From October

Why it matters

The county's own budget shows two thirds of spending goes on running the government and one third on building anything. That is an official, checkable figure and nobody in this race is using it.

Meanwhile a false claim about your salary is circulating with specific figures attached. A sourced graphic ends that; a denial does not.

What gets built

  • County finance explainers — where the money goes, drawn from Controller of Budget records
  • Programme charts showing coverage by ward and progress by phase
  • Correction graphics that answer a circulating false figure with the gazetted one
  • The film's end cards, where each closing figure carries its source on screen
Timeline
Produced as needed. Each piece is days, not weeks.
What I need
Nothing you do not already have. These are built from public records.
Caution
Only figures that can be sourced get published. A wrong chart is worse than no chart.
Done looks like

An argument about county spending that fits on a phone screen and cannot be disputed.

Sheet 07  ·  Research and verification

Verification

Keeping every claim traceable, and knowing what is being said before it becomes a story.

Ongoing
Started already

Why it matters

Your own figures currently drift. Scholarships appear as both 409 and 449; the target as 3,500, 4,000 and 120 per ward; the programme under two different names. Each of those is something an opponent can hold up.

Fourteen Nandi groups were read from the inside for this work. It surfaced a second opinion poll nobody had cited, an elders' endorsement for a rival, and what a disputed Kalenjin word actually means.

What gets built

  • One agreed figure set, pinned and used everywhere
  • A source file behind every published claim, so anything can be defended within the hour
  • Monitoring of what is circulating, including the groups that are not searchable from outside
  • Early warning on a story before it has run for three days
Timeline
Already underway, unpaid, so the work can be judged before anything is agreed.
What I need
Programme records and access to the people who ran each initiative.
Limit
This is verification, not polling and not legal advice. Both of those need specialists.
Done looks like

Every number the campaign says in public can be traced to a document within a minute.
