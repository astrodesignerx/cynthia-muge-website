01

The premise, and the trap in it

Sixteen gentle films about coffee farmers will not do 216,000 plays. If that is all this series is, it will underperform, and the campaign will conclude that evidence does not travel. That conclusion would be wrong, and avoiding it is the whole design problem.

What actually travels on her material is her, speaking unscripted, in Nandi, with force, in front of a crowd. That is not a preference, it is what the numbers say. So the series does not choose between her voice and the evidence. It uses one to carry the other.

The encouraging finding

Her own best post carries a 6.4% like rate (5,178 on 80,500) — matching @aldai001's 6.4% and beating @k.entertainment.digital's 5.2%. People who see her material engage with it at least as hard as they engage with anyone else's cut of it. Her problem is distribution, not appeal. That is a far cheaper problem to fix, and captions fix most of it.

And the trap in the top row

The 0.70% share rate belongs to a video criticising her. Controversy shares harder than anything else in this dataset, and those are hostile shares. Do not chase that number. Target 0.25% on evidence content — roughly double her current baseline, achieved by people who agree with what they are passing on.

What each short has to do

  1. Fix the caption gap in week one. Her captioned posts do 80,500. Her captionless ones do 802. This is free reach and it needs no approval from anyone.
  2. Build a visual identity she does not currently have. Sixteen shorts with one grammar, one audio mark and one fact-card format will make her recognisable at a glance for the first time.
  3. End on something checkable. Every short closes on a number with its source named on screen. Over sixteen weeks that becomes a habit the audience notices, and it is the direct answer to the unsourced round numbers on her website.
  4. Reach the 54% who have not decided. Supporter content performs comfortably and changes nothing. Aim past the people already commenting "our incoming governor".
  5. Land the film. By mid-January the audience should already know these farmers by name.

One template, held for all sixteen. Consistency is what turns a run of clips into a series, and the series is what makes the last one land.

1080 × 1920, 9:16. Shoot 4K 16:9 and reframe, or shoot native vertical for the pieces you know are vertical-only. Never letterbox a horizontal clip.

Keep every word inside roughly x 100–880, y 250–1400. App UI eats the top strip, the bottom quarter and the right rail. Verify against the current app build before locking the template.

Two frames of the cherry-into-bucket impact from shot 1.5. Same on every short. This is the audio mark and it costs nothing.
Hook
By 0:03. A face mid-sentence, a hand doing something, or a question on screen. Never a logo, never a slow establishing wide.
Subtitles
Burned in, always. Nandi speech subtitled in English. Most people watch muted, and this is not optional on any short.
Fact card
Last four to five seconds. Plain type on black. One number, one line of source. No short ships without one.
Music
Only under process sequences. Never under a payment, a document or a failure.
Naming
NW[##]_[slug]_[platform]_v[n].mp4 — e.g. NW08_docket_TT_v2.mp4. Archive every version.

The one rule that governs the rest

One idea per short. The temptation on every single one of these will be to add a second point. Resist it. A short that makes one claim and proves it will outperform a short that makes three and proves none, and the sixteen together make the full argument anyway.

03

The sixteen

Four movements. The arc runs craft → evidence → argument → case, and it is deliberate: the audience has to trust the series before it will accept the political shorts in December and January.

Shot references point to The Tree in Her Name. Most of this material is captured anyway during principal photography — the series costs edit time, not shoot days.

The reactive lane, running underneath all sixteen

The scheduled series is weekly. Capture of her speaking is continuous. Every public appearance, church visit and rally gets filmed in full and a clip goes out the same day — 30 to 60 seconds, captioned, from her own account.

This is the fix for the biggest structural problem in the audit: other people currently own her best moments and out-reach her three to twenty times using her own footage. Same-day publishing takes that away from them. It also means that when the next controversy comes, the version circulating is hers with context rather than someone else's thirty-second excerpt.

Target turnaround: four hours from event to post. That requires someone with an edit machine at the event, not a weekly batch.

04

Caption system

The cheapest measurable win available to this campaign. Her captioned posts run at 80,500 plays; her uncaptioned ones bottom out at 802. Nothing else in this document is that cheap.

TikTok — short, front-loaded, tags carry the reach
Line 1 — the hook restated, or a question. This is all that shows before "more". She planted these in 2023. Look at them now. Line 2 — one line of context. Optional. Kahawa na Mama, Nandi Hills. #cynthiamuge #nandi #kalenjintiktok #kenyantiktok🇰🇪 #kahawanamama #coffeekenya #fyp
Facebook — longer works, and the audience is closer to her
Line 1 — same hook. Facebook shows more, but earns nothing by burying it. She planted these in 2023. Look at them now. Lines 2–4 — the context TikTok has no room for. Name the ward. Name the person. Mama [name] received 200 seedlings in the pilot year at [ward]. This week she delivered her first cherry to the society. Line 5 — the fact, with its source. Always. 650,000+ seedlings have gone to about 4,000 women across all six sub-counties since 2023. (Kenya News Agency, June 2025) #KahawaNaMama #Nandi

Rules

  • Never post without a caption. This is the entire finding. No exceptions, including for reactive clips.
  • Do not cross-post identically. Same video, different caption register. TikTok is reaching strangers; Facebook is reaching people who already know her.
  • Name people. "A woman in Nandi Hills" is worth less than "Mama [name], Kilibwoni". Named beneficiaries are the whole credibility argument — with consent, every time.
  • Source every number in the caption, not only on the fact card. It costs six words and it is the habit that separates her from every competitor.
  • Tag sets: keep a fixed core of three (#cynthiamuge #nandi #kahawanamama) and rotate four situational ones. Fixed tags build a searchable body of work; rotating tags chase reach.
  • Do not use #cynthiamugeforgovernor on official accounts before nomination. Let supporters carry it. On her own channel it reads as presumption and feeds the project tag.
05

Distribution

Posting is half the job. Nandi's political argument happens inside Facebook groups — county forums, constituency forums, ward forums — and a post on her page does not reach them automatically. Someone has to carry it in.

The chain

  1. Publish to TikTok and her Facebook page, Thursday, same asset, different captions.
  2. Seed to the office page within the hour.
  3. Distribute to a standing list of ward coordinators with the video file and a suggested caption, so it enters group conversations as a person sharing something rather than a page advertising.
  4. Diaspora — the Australia networks activated in June are a live list. Give them the file and the dates.
  5. Archive to YouTube monthly as a batch, so the material is searchable and permanent.

Build the coordinator list in September

One contact per ward, thirty wards, on WhatsApp. WhatsApp is the most-used platform in Kenya by self-declared use and it is invisible to every analytics dashboard, which is exactly why it matters. Without this list the series reaches her existing followers and stops. With it, sixteen shorts enter thirty ward conversations sixteen times.

What to do with the numbers

  • Weeks 1–4 set the baseline. Do not judge any single short before week five. Four data points is not a pattern.
  • If completion is low, fix the first three seconds — not the topic. Almost every underperforming short is a hook problem wearing a subject-matter disguise.
  • If share rate stays flat but likes are healthy, the content is comforting supporters rather than convincing anyone. Push harder toward the evidence shorts.
  • Do not chase a viral outlier. One short will overperform for reasons nobody can reproduce. Note it, do not restructure the series around it.
07

What not to make

Out of scope for this series, without exception

  • No response to the August 2026 remarks controversy. Wrong vehicle entirely. A vertical short cannot carry it, and attempting one reopens the story for an audience that has moved on.
  • No content touching circumcision, initiation, or cultural standing. There is no winning move on that ground and this series should never set foot on it.
  • No opponent named. Not Kosgey, not Melly, not Lagat. She is behind — attacking upward raises them and confirms she is the challenger.
  • Nothing about the Kimasas land allegation. Unverified in the research. Repeating it puts her on the wrong side of an evidence-based positioning she is otherwise building.
  • No round unsourced numbers. No "100,000 lives impacted". The entire value of this series is that its numbers can be checked.
  • No AI voiceover, no stock music, no trending-audio lip-sync. The audio mark and locally recorded score are the identity. Borrowed audio erases it.
  • No "incoming governor" framing on official channels before nomination. Supporters can say it. She cannot.

Two judgement calls to make in September, not December

  1. NW12, "The Ones That Died", ships. Get it agreed in writing before the shoot, alongside the film's Scene 14. In December it will be argued about; in September it costs nothing to agree.
  2. Who holds the account. Not her. Her job is to speak. A production team captures, edits, captions and publishes — the gap between an 80,500-play post and an 802-play post is entirely operational, and it will not close by asking her to post more.
